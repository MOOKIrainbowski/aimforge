Firing error

Hip fire costs you

Every weapon has its own error from the hip and when aimed, blended by how far into the sights you are. The sniper misses by half a metre unscoped and is exact through the scope; the SMG barely cares either way.

Bloom

Holding the trigger has a price

Sustained fire walks the shot off the crosshair and recovers when you let go. The first shot after any pause is always clean, so tapping stays a pure test of aim.

Recoil

A pattern worth learning

Each automatic has a pattern that runs its whole magazine — climb, walk to one side, swing back. Recoil Control mode scores how well you cancel it, not just whether you hit.

Targets

Spheres to measure with, figures to practise on

A sphere is the same size from every angle, which makes it an honest yardstick. Switch to humanoid targets when you want zones — and headshots that get called out as you land them.

Feel

Everything you hear is generated

No audio files. Each weapon's report is synthesized from its own layers — the blast's leading edge, the crack, the gas, the low punch you feel, the tail leaving the room, and the action working a beat later. Brass ejects, arcs and settles on the floor. Nothing to download.

Measurement

The part that makes it training

Shooting is the easy half. What matters is what the session tells you afterwards.

Coach feedback, not a tip list

Rule-based tips compare a session against your own trend: flick bias, tracking consistency, reaction spread, how much of the recoil you actually cancelled.

History and hit maps

Every session is kept and charted, with a heatmap of where your shots land relative to target centre — the fastest way to see a consistent bias.

Real sensitivity conversion

Enter your DPI and cm/360 and AimonSite matches it across Valorant, CS2, Apex, Overwatch 2 and Fortnite, so practice transfers to the game you actually play.

Your crosshair, exactly

Shape, size, thickness, gap, outline, centre dot and colour — set once and used everywhere, with target colour kept separate so contrast is yours to choose.

A range you can tune

Wall and floor colour, brightness, field of view, and whether magazines and reloads exist at all. Off by default: an aim trainer should not interrupt you to reload.

A desktop build too

The same trainer as a lightweight desktop app, with higher-fidelity shadows, bloom and reflections for practice offline.

How it works

From click to coach feedback in about a minute

1

Pick a drill, then a weapon

Choose the mode, difficulty and length, then pick what you are carrying on the way in — each one shoots, reloads and cycles differently.

2

Step into the range

Click to lock your mouse and shoot. Raw input, sampled at full rate and applied once per frame, so a fast turn stays smooth.

3

Read what happened

Score, accuracy, headshots, time-to-kill and coach tips straight after, then your trend over every session you have run.

Private by default

Your stats stay on your machine

Every session, setting and crosshair preset lives in your browser's own storage. There is no account to make and nothing to upload — and signing in does not change that.
